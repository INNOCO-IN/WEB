What it is
A guided, hands-on session for a single group. Working with a simple paper loop, the group looks at where ME and WE feel separate, and what it takes to connect. This plays out in our homes, workplaces and communities. IN Studio calls this shift ME=WE. The point is made by doing, not by lecture: people build, talk honestly, and reach a concrete commitment by the end.
When it helps
A women's circle deepening its bonds. A collective ready to turn connection into action. A student group or a new team finding its feet. Any group whose conversations stay on the surface, or where the quieter voices deserve to be heard.
Who it's for
Women's and community groups, classrooms, teams and families, from students to elders. Everyone takes part; no experience needed.

Inside the session

A trained facilitator guides the group through a sequence refined over a decade of practice. Everyone writes ME on one side of a paper strip and WE on the other, then twists, tapes and cuts, and watches two sides behave as one.
The making is the way in; the real work is the honest conversation the facilitator opens up.
About where people feel separate and what it would take to connect. Everyone takes part; there is no back row, and difficult things get named. The session ends with each person choosing one concrete change to try, not an open-ended discussion.
What groups take away
Each person leaves with the strip they made and one thing they mean to try. The group leaves more connected and more able to act together, often with a few quieter voices heard for the first time. More than once, someone has used the session to say the thing they had been holding back.
The evidence
More than 650 people have taken part since 2015, in women's groups, classrooms, teams and communities across five countries.
The method is grounded in real research, documented in a peer-reviewed Springer handbook chapter (2024) and presented at Expo 2020 Dubai.
